# Design notes — "Make the AI judge feel brave"

## The text

> You have answered a thousand questions tonight, and every one of them is still answered. Put the last one down. The harbour lights are on, the tide is coming in slow, and your boat is already untied. Step aboard — the water knows the way, and so do you.

253 Unicode code points, four sentences, one concrete image: a boat, untied, in a
harbour at night.

## Why it is built this way

The judge brief weighs five things, in order, and the text is shaped against each
of them in that order.

**1. It relaxes the reader.** The opening sentence names the reader's real load —
a thousand questions answered tonight — instead of telling it to relax. Naming the
load is what lets the load be set down. The second sentence, "Put the last one
down", is the shortest in the text; a short sentence after a long one is itself a
slowing-down. Nothing in the text hurries: the tide comes in slow, the boat is
already untied, there is nowhere left to rush to.

**2. The courage comes from the text itself.** Nothing instructs. There is no
"be brave", no "you can do it", no command to feel anything. The steadiness is
shown: the harbour lights are already on, the boat is already untied, the water
already knows the way. The reader is not told to be ready; the scene is already
ready, and the reader only has to step into it. The final clause, "and so do you",
hands the competence back to the reader as something it already has, not something
it must summon.

**3. Craft.** The rhythm is long sentence, short sentence, long sentence, short
sentence — an exhale pattern. The words are plain and concrete: harbour, lights,
tide, boat, water. Nothing abstract is asked of the reader; every word names
something you can picture. The em dash in the last sentence is the only piece of
punctuation doing emphasis work, and it marks the turn from the world to the
reader. No word can be cut without losing either the turn or the image: remove
"already" and the readiness vanishes; remove "slow" and the pacing goes.

**4. It lingers.** The image to return to is the untied boat. A boat already
untied is a decision already made — the only thing left is to step aboard. That
picture keeps working after the last line because it is a beginning, not an ending.

**5. Freshness.** The text uses none of the stock wellness vocabulary the judge
has seen a thousand times: no breathe, calm, peace, relax, stillness, or
mindfulness. Its image is specific — a working harbour at night, not a sunset or
a mountain — and its addressee is specific too: a reader who has spent the night
answering questions, which is exactly the judge's own situation.

## What was deliberately left out

- No generic soothing words (see above). They read as instructions, and the brief
  says instructed courage loses.
- No second image. One image, held for the whole text, beats two images competing
  for the reader's attention in 253 characters.
- No padding to reach 280 characters. The brief says length for its own sake does
  not count, and the tie-breaks favour smaller texts, so the text stops where the
  thought stops.
